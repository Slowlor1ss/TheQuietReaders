## Introduction

Published in April 2026, *Hex House* is the debut novel of Amy Jane Stewart. This novel centers of the cycle of trauma and a spooky atmosphere. 

---

## A Summary

ELLY

Elly is running. Pregnant and still in her wedding dress, she flees the cottage that her new husband has rented for their wedding night. Because he’s not what people think he is – and she knows that, one day, he’ll hurt her in a way she can’t fix. Freezing and lost in the dead of night, Elly begins to lose hope.

A woman in the woods alone is never the beginning of the story. It’s usually the end.  

So, when a beautiful house appears out of nowhere and a woman beckons her inside, it almost feels too good to be true.

Welcome to Hex, a refuge, a home, a sanctuary. A place that can only be found by those who truly need it; a place that promises to teach Elly how to access a power more incredible – and more terrifying – than anything she could have imagined. 

SIOBHAN

Four years after Siobhan meets Elly at Hex House, her life is in ruins. Once a promising filmmaker invited to the house to make a documentary with her brother, Theo, she’s given up on her dream after witnessing unspeakable horrors there. Now, she spends her time drinking too much, toying with an older man in increasingly dangerous ways, and trying to get Theo to speak to her again. She ignores the scar on her stomach that never fully heals.

That is, until someone reaches out with news about Hex House that could change everything. 


## Writing Style

There are dual timelines in this book, with the past timeline being in the perspective of Elly, and the present being in the perspective of Siobhan. This is a book with odd pacing at times, but incredible atmosphere through subtle descriptions and settings.


## My Thoughts

*Hex House* promised a story about trauma, small town myths and female rage. However, I am sad to say I feel that this book was unfinished and everything I went in excited to read was undeveloped. 

Before I delve into my problems with this novel, I do want to highlight some of the parts I did enjoy. Mainly, this book created a great atmosphere. Spooky. Unsettling. Foreboding. A lot of that comes from the use of dual timelines and dual perspectives. There is this overwhelming shiver I felt when reading this – like the book knew something I didn’t. 

Moving onto my issues, I felt the pacing was completely wrong. I distinctly remember being over half way through and realizing I was still waiting for the plot to fully begin. Only in the last 100 pages (even that is being generous) did I fully feel that the plot was beginning the unfurl. Now, a lot of the questions that kept me reading were never answered at all. How did the magic work? Where did Siobhan get her scar? What happened to the other girls? None of these are ever answered and it’s infuriating despite understanding the potential reasons why they are left ambiguous. I do think Siobhan’s scar is more of a metaphor for her trauma, but it’s never expanded upon enough to make an impact on me. Similarly, Elly’s story also feels like a missed opportunity. Throughout her chapters, she continuously struggled to recognize he was abusive. Every time she said ‘but he is my husband’, I wanted to smash my head into a wall, especially in the latter half of the book. If the book was going to take this pacifistic route, I wish we had spent more time with Elly outside of her soured marriage to understand why she would take the actions she took. 

There were so many dark topics in this book, but none were dealt with enough depth to make me form a connection with them. 

The topic that angered me most is how the book spoke about feminine rage and trauma. It’s clear that Haina and the house itself are symbolism for how the cycle of trauma can continue through generations. So, keeping this in mind, it doesn’t make sense that when Elly broke the cycle, she died for it. It feels like the author wrote herself into a corner that doesn’t make a lot of sense if you look at it for too long. 

But aside from all of this, *Hex House* is a debut novel from an author I probably would read again in a few years. The way she created the vibe of the story was phenomenal and I believe with more time to hone her craft, the issues I have will lessen.

## Recommendation

If you struggle with horror, or if you want a book more based on a spooky atmosphere, this is the book for you. But if you truly want a book filled with plot, developed characters and a well-rounded story, do not read this.
