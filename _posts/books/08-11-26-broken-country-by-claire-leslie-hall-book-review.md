## Introduction

Published in 2025 by Claire Leslie Hall, *Broken Country* tells the story about how love can transcend life itself, and how loss can permanently morph someone into something else entirely. 

---

## A Summary

Beth was seventeen when she first met Gabriel. Over that heady, intense summer, he made her think and feel and see differently. She thought it was the start of her great love story. When Gabriel left to become the person his mother expected him to be, she was broken.

It was Frank who picked up the pieces and together they built a home very different from the one she'd imagined with Gabriel. Watching her husband and son, she remembered feeling so sure that, after everything, this was the life she was supposed to be leading.

But when Gabriel comes back, all Beth's certainty about who she is and what she wants crumbles. Even after ten years, their connection is instant. She knows it's wrong and she knows people could get hurt. But how can she resist a second chance at first love? 


## My Thoughts

*Broken Country* promised a story about love, mistakes and the intricacies of grief. However, it delivered something entirely different. The praise I have seen for this book is seemingly endless, but I just cannot summon the same excitement regarding this novel. I have many complaints, but I want to go over what I did enjoy first for your understanding.

I truly enjoyed the prose. It maintains a constant weight over the entire story, not allowing the reader to forget how heavily the death affects Beth, and it does so beautifully. Hall has a particular skill to turn a normal setting - such as a farm - into a once-in-a-lifetime piece of scenery more perfect than can be captured by the human eye. I also loved the use of the past and present timeline to create suspense and add to the heartbreak that was ever looming over the narrative.

Now onto my not-so-nice criticism of the book. I found the characters, specifically Beth, insufferable, and not even just because of the infidelity. Repeatedly, she makes painfully stupid decisions, with no thought or care, and then is surprised when she receives consequences for them. Obviously this is most prevalent in infidelity, but I also found her decision to have a child with Frank while he was on trial for murder to be absolutely insane. It’s painted as a joyous choice, but she did just sentence a child to have no father for the first 7 years of their life simply because she wanted to. That really nailed the coffin for me to say that there was no character arc for Beth from start to end. She was selfish and heartbroken at the start, and she was the same at the end (albeit a little less sad). Beth is the main character, and yet it felt as if her arc was not prioritized over the turns and twists.

Onto the portrayal of love, I could understand what Hall was trying to do, but I didn’t feel it at all. Beth’s love with Gabriel is representative of the young love that most of us deal with. Filled equally with butterflies, lust and tears, their love perfectly shows how choices in your youth don’t disappear simply because you got older. This part is fine. I think it could have been a little more whimsical in its portrayal but even as it is, it works. The main issue is the love between Beth and Frank. I believe Hall was trying to create a more stable type of love. More so quiet dinners than extravagant dates. But not once did I truly feel like she loved Frank. She *told* me she loved him, but her actions continually proved otherwise. There is so much potential complexity for this that gets entirely looked over for no good reason.

Overall, I really wanted to love this, but I just could not get over the blinding issues with it.

## Recommendation

Everyone else I know adored this book, so I am hesitant to give my opinion on whether or not you should personally read this. If the issues I stated above are deal breakers for you, I’d give it a miss, but otherwise - I hope you enjoy!
