## Introduction

Published in 2022 by Abigail Bergstrom, *What a Shame* is **the** weird girl lit-fic to satisfy your desire to read about shame, grief and friendship. With incredibly written female protagonists that you can surely relate to, it’s a wonder this debut novel didn’t blow up when it was released.

---

## A Summary

The idea of a curse was divisive, but the assertion that I had, for some time now, been 'laden with something dark' was disconcertingly unanimous.

I wondered if this was something you also saw in me, if that was why you left.

There is something wrong with Mathilda.

She's still reeling from the blow of a gut-punch break up and grieving the death of a loved one.

But that's not it.

She's cried all her tears, mastered her crow pose and thrown out every last reminder of him.

But that's not helping.

Concerned that she isn't moving on, Mathilda's friends push her towards a series of increasingly unorthodox remedies.

Until the seams of herself begin to come undone. 


## My Thoughts

I had never heard of this book before picking it up off of a Waterstones shelf and taking it home with me. I went into it knowing nothing, and I honestly feel like I know less about the book now that I have finished it. 

This book covers a wide range of themes, but it doesn’t do any of them as in depth as I would have liked. For example, the theme of friendship is strong throughout this, with the main characters all being friends with Mathilda. This extends to Constance as well, who I will discuss more later. I loved this particular aspect of the book, with each of them having such strong personalities, their own issues that are actually explored and a general feel that they had their own lives outside of what the novel showed. However, despite all of this goodness, I struggled immensely to remember who was who in the first half of the book. This was especially true with Eden and Ekua, who I constantly got mixed up with one another.  

Moving into my next issue – Eden’s character as a whole felt unnecessary. Don’t get me wrong, I grew to like her personality and it added some more humor, but I feel like she could have been written to be one of the roommates (or at least not live in Paris) or even just merging her personality with another of the characters. As it stands now, her existence feels untied to the plot and a tad bit removed.

The next major theme that I want to cover is grief. Grief is, I would say, the central plot of the book. I think this was overcomplicated in *What a Shame* to the extent I found it confusing. Mathilda lost both her drunk, abusive father, and got left by her boyfriend. At the beginning of most chapters, there is this monologue in which Mathilda discusses the loss and things that remind her of them. However, this was written so poorly that I struggled to understand who she was talking about. These parts of the book fall way too far into a direction that the book just isn’t going, leading to a confusing reading experience. I will say, the saving grace is the character of Constance. This little old lady was my favorite part, and her existence allows Mathilda to see grief in a different light. 

The final theme to discuss is trauma. I actually liked the way Mathilda is written in terms of how her trauma manifested. There are plenty of hints in the pages about what happened to her as a child, but it is kept vague until her ayahuasca trip towards the end. As a reader, I could see how her unhealed trauma followed her in every aspect, whether it be love, connection, work or happiness.

Overall, I think, for a debut novel, this was an interesting look into grief, but the author needs to take more time to distinguish her voice in literature. I am excited to read her future novels.
